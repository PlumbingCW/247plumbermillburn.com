A faucet or valve that will not shut off

When a faucet, valve or sink supply line will not shut off, the call for faucet repair in Millburn, NJ is the 24/7 dispatch line, and the first move is yours. Turn the oval handles under the sink clockwise to close the fixture stops. If they spin loosely or leak, close the main water valve, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house.

Afterward, open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the pipes and ease the pressure, set a bucket under any drip, and keep cords and outlets out of the water. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and after-hours calls reach the same licensed plumbers.

Shutting off water at the fixture stop and at the main

  1. Find the fixture stops

    Look under the sink for two small valves on the lines running up to the faucet.

  2. Turn them slowly clockwise

    Stop when the handle resists, since forcing an old stop can snap the stem.

  3. Go to the main if a stop fails

    The main is usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house.

  4. Open the lowest faucet

    This drains the pipes and relieves pressure so the leak slows.

  5. Call the 24/7 line

    Tell the dispatcher which valve is stuck, leaking or broken.

A broken supply valve is why a small fixture repair becomes an emergency. Stops go years without being turned, and then the stem cracks or the valve seizes open. Faucet, valve and fixture repair

Drips, sprays and stiff handles

A drip from the spout usually means a worn washer, seat, O-ring or cartridge inside the faucet. A spray from under the handle points to a failed O-ring or a loose packing nut, and a stiff handle often means scale around the cartridge. Most of these are repairs, not replacements, and a plumber can often finish in one visit when parts allow.

Leaks under the sink

Wet cabinet floors usually trace to a supply line connection, a pull-down hose coupling or the drain. Dry every joint with a towel, run the water and feel for the first wet spot. A leak that keeps returning after a fixture repair may be inside the wall, and that is where leak detection earns its keep.

Weak flow and clogged aerators

Low flow from one faucet only is typically a clogged aerator or cartridge, not a pressure problem. Mineral grit builds up in the screen, and cleaning or replacing it restores the stream. If every fixture runs weak, the cause lies elsewhere in the house.

Showers and tubs

A leaking shower valve or a showerhead that drips after the water is off usually comes down to a worn cartridge or seat behind the handle. A dripping tub spout and a leaking tub faucet follow the same pattern, and the handles come apart to reach the stem. If water appears in the ceiling below, stop using the shower and call.

A shower or tub that drains slowly is usually holding hair and soap scum in the strainer or trap. Pull the strainer and clear what you can see. When the water still pools around your ankles, the clog is farther along the line, and drain cleaning reaches it.

Turn your stops once a year

Open and close each fixture stop gently once or twice a year so it does not freeze in place. A stop that moves is a stop that works when you need it.
