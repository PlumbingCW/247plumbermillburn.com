Why we only take calls

There are no online forms, and that is on purpose. A leak does not wait for an inbox. On a call, the dispatcher can tell you where the main valve usually is, ask whether anyone smells gas, and give safety steps in the moment.

A phone call is also quicker for sorting a real emergency from a job that can wait. One conversation covers the symptom, the address and the next step. Back-and-forth messages cost time when water is moving.

How to verify any New Jersey plumber

Plumbing contractors in New Jersey are licensed through the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, which is part of the Division of Consumer Affairs. You can check any plumber with the board, including ours. A trustworthy company will not mind.

  1. Ask for the license details

    Get the plumber's name and license information before work begins.

  2. Look them up with the board

    Use the Division of Consumer Affairs listing for the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers.

  3. Match the details

    Confirm that the name and status match the person at your door.

  4. Ask about insurance

    Request proof of coverage if the job is large or inside a finished space.

Why local flooding history shapes our questions

Millburn has flooded before, in Hurricane Floyd in 1999, in Hurricane Irene in 2011 and on the night of September 1, 2021, when the West Branch of the Rahway River overflowed after Ida. That history shapes the questions a dispatcher asks during a storm, such as whether the sump pit is rising or the basement floor drain is bubbling.

Those answers help sort a pump problem from a sewer problem. They also tell the plumber what to bring to the job before the visit begins.
