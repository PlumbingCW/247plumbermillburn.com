Hot water that turns cold mid-shower, or a unit showing an error

The shower is fine for a minute, then it goes lukewarm or cold, or the display flashes a code and the unit shuts down. People often assume the heater is dying. More often it is scale on the heat exchanger, a clogged inlet screen, a flow problem or a vent fault.

Tankless water heater service in Millburn, NJ starts by working out which of those it is. This page covers flushing, the faults that show up most, and how to decide between repair and replacement.

Why scale builds up in a tankless unit

A tankless unit heats water as it passes through a small heat exchanger, so every drop meets very hot metal. Dissolved minerals come out of the water when it is heated and stick to that metal. NJ American Water's 2025 report for the Short Hills System lists total hardness from 52 to 342 mg/L, so your tap can differ from a neighbor's.

Scale narrows the passages, so the unit works harder, overheats, throws error codes and finally shuts down. Water arrives lukewarm as flow drops. Test your tap with a hardness strip or a lab test, since the report does not say which streets get which value.

A flush reverses the problem. The plumber closes the isolation valves, connects hoses and a small pump, circulates a descaling solution through the exchanger, then rinses. Units installed without isolation valves need them added first. The right interval depends on your water and the maker's manual.

Install, repair or replace a tankless water heater

A tankless unit far from the end of its life is usually serviced, not replaced. Scale, a dirty filter, a failed sensor or igniter and a blocked vent are parts and cleaning jobs, and repairing them makes sense on a younger unit.

Replacement enters the picture when the heat exchanger itself has failed or leaks, when repeated codes follow every repair, or when parts for an older model are hard to find. Age and brand matter, and an honest plumber will tell you which side of the line your unit is on.

A new install is a different project. Tankless units often need a larger gas line than a tank, a vent run, an electrical outlet and sometimes a condensate drain. Water heater installation covers that planning, and water heater repair covers a storage tank that is giving trouble.

Ask about your brand

Some plumbers repair only certain brands of tankless unit, and parts and software differ between makers. Give the dispatcher the brand and model on the data plate, and ask directly whether that unit can be serviced before the visit is set.

What a tankless service visit includes

  1. Check the symptoms and codes

    The history of the problem and any stored codes point toward the cause.

  2. Inspect inlet, vent and flow

    The inlet screen, the vent run and flow at the fixtures are checked.

  3. Flush and descale

    Isolation valves are closed and a descaling solution is circulated through the exchanger.

  4. Test the unit

    Ignition, temperature and the condensate drain are tested under flow.

  5. Advise on what is next

    You hear whether the unit is a repair candidate or is nearing replacement.
