At home, the usual trouble is the main drain. Signs the blockage is in the main line

  • Gurgling in a tub or floor drain when a toilet is flushed
  • More than one fixture draining slowly at the same time
  • Water or sewage showing in the lowest drain in the building
  • A sewer smell that returns shortly after the drain is plunged

While you wait for the plumber, stop sending water into the blocked line. Turn off the dishwasher and washing machine, leave the toilets unflushed, and lift anything valuable off the floor near the lowest drain.

Before we arrive in Livingston

Shut off the main water valve

In a house, the main valve is usually where the line enters the basement or utility closet. In a shop or mall unit, ask the landlord or property manager where the tenant shutoff sits. Close it if water is still flowing from a broken pipe or fixture.

Cut power near wet areas

If water is spreading toward outlets, a floor-level panel or a water heater, switch off those circuits only when you can reach the breaker without standing in water. Otherwise stay out and tell the dispatcher.

Clear the area and protect valuables

Lift boxes, rugs, stock and electronics off wet floors. In a retail unit, keep staff and customers away from the wet zone so nobody slips, and block off restrooms that have overflowed.
