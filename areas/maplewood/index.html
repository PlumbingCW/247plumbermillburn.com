Before we arrive in Maplewood

Shut off the main water valve

If a pipe has failed during the storm, close the main valve where the line enters the house. If the problem is only rain and groundwater coming in, the valve will not stop it, but it helps rule out a supply line.

Cut power near wet areas

Do not step into standing water near an outlet, a freezer or the panel. If you can reach the breaker from dry ground, switch off the basement circuits. Otherwise stay upstairs and tell the dispatcher what is wet.

Clear the area and protect valuables

A year of weather, a year of calls

Spring thaws and summer thunderstorms test sump pumps, and heavy leaf fall in autumn clogs the gutters and drains that carry water away from the foundation. Winter brings a different problem, because pipes in unheated spaces can freeze and split once temperatures fall far enough.

A pump that runs constantly in wet weather is working hard, and its float switch or check valve can fail without warning. Our sump pump installation and repair work covers both a new pit and a worn pump, and a quick test now costs less time than a flooded basement later.

  1. Look at the pit

    Check for debris, silt or a stuck float in the sump pit.

  2. Pour in water

    Add a bucket of water and watch for the pump to start and empty the pit.

  3. Check the discharge

    Make sure the outlet pipe outside is clear and sends water away from the house.

  4. Call if it hesitates

    A pump that hums, cycles often or stays silent needs a plumber before the next storm.

Signs a sump pump is on its way out

  • It runs nonstop in wet weather or cycles every few seconds
  • It makes grinding, rattling or humming noises
  • It is more than a few years old and has never been tested
  • The float sticks or the pit never fully empties
