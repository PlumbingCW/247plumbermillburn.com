Town by town, in plumbing terms

Livingston had 31,330 residents in the 2020 Census and runs its own municipal water department. Homes near the Route 10 corridor and around Northfield Center get the same dispatch, but the water report you need comes from the township, not from NJ American Water.

West Orange mixes Victorian, Jazz Age and Tudor houses with garden apartments and post-war homes, so pipe material changes from one house to the next. NJ American Water replaced a 1930s cast iron main on Eagle Rock Avenue, which shows how old parts of the underground system are. Ask the plumber to identify your own lines.

Maplewood has the East Branch of the Rahway River running through it and South Mountain Reservation on its border, so ask about a sump pump if your basement has taken on water. NJ American Water also announced replacing about 200 feet of 12-inch cast iron lines from the 1920s on Oakland Road.

Springfield is crossed by I-78, US 22, Route 24 and Route 124, and NJ American Water announced upgrades there worth $1.6 million in August 2026. After street work, run a cold tap until the water clears before you suspect your own plumbing.

Summit counted 8,365 housing units in the 2020 Census, around a historic downtown district. NJ American Water announced replacing about 2.4 miles of aging main, with lines going back to the 1920s. That is utility work, so report street-side problems to the utility and anything inside the house to us.

What changes from town to town

Millburn, West Orange, Maplewood, Springfield and Summit are all served by NJ American Water. Livingston is the exception, because the Township of Livingston publishes its own annual water report and its Water Department handles copies. If you live there, ask the department for the current report before you judge your water.

The county line matters less than people expect, but it is worth knowing. Springfield and Summit are in Union County, while Livingston, West Orange and Maplewood are in Essex. Permit rules vary, so ask your own town's building department before work that may need approval.

Pipe ownership also affects repairs. In Millburn, NJ American Water began a lead service line replacement program in August 2024. The utility owns the line from the main to the curb stop, and the property owner owns the rest into the building. Ask your own utility how it handles this before hiring anyone for repiping.
