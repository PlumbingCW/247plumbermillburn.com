Before we arrive in Springfield

Shut off the main water valve

If a water heater or supply line is leaking, close the main valve where water enters the house, or the cold supply valve above the heater. A garden apartment or condo may have a unit valve, so look under sinks and ask the manager.

Cut power near wet areas

For an electric water heater, switch off its breaker if you can reach it from dry ground. For a gas unit, do not touch anything if you smell gas. Leave, move well away and call the gas utility or 911.

Clear the area and protect valuables

Move storage boxes, holiday decorations and laundry away from the heater and floor drain. Water spreads further than it looks, so lift anything that would soak up damp from the floor.

When a stubborn drain needs more than a snake

A cable punches a hole through a blockage. Hydro-jetting uses pressurized water to scrub grease, scale and silt off the pipe wall, which is why it fits lines that clog repeatedly. It does not suit every pipe, and a camera inspection first tells us whether the line can take it.

Homes with repeat main-line backups often end up here. For lines that have already failed, sewer line repair follows once the camera shows where.

How a jetting visit usually runs

  1. Locate a cleanout or access point and look at the line with a camera.
  2. Flush the pipe with controlled high-pressure water from the cleanout toward the main.
  3. Run the camera again to confirm the wall is clear.
  4. Explain what we saw, and whether the line needs repair or just regular upkeep.

A tank that is failing, and the choice to replace

Water heaters give warnings. Rust-colored hot water, rumbling from scale on the bottom of the tank, moisture around the base and a pilot that will not stay lit all point toward the end. A leak from the tank itself is not repairable, and the answer is water heater replacement.

A heater in a finished basement or utility closet carries higher stakes, because a failed tank floods a room that holds other things. If you hear dripping or see a puddle, shut the cold supply, kill the power or gas, and call.
