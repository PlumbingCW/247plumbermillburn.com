The two calls Summit makes most

Someone searching for an emergency plumber in Summit, NJ is usually dealing with one of two things. Either a water heater has failed and flooded a utility room, or an old supply pipe has sprung another leak. Our licensed plumbers dispatch from Millburn, NJ around the clock, and they ask which of those it is before they leave.

Summit is a city in Union County, with 22,719 residents counted in 2020 and 8,365 housing units. Most of those units have a water heater and a network of supply lines, so the demand is steady and rarely seasonal.

Repiping, and when patching has run its course

A single pinhole leak is a repair. A second and a third in different rooms is a pattern, and the pipe itself is the problem. At that point repiping replaces worn supply lines in planned sections, so you are not cutting into a different wall every year.

Summit's water utility is also working on its end. NJ American Water announced in March 2025 that it was replacing about 2.4 miles of aging main, with lines installed as far back as the 1920s. Street work is the utility's job, while the pipe inside your walls and the line from the curb stop to your house are a separate matter.

Signs your supply lines may be due for replacement

  • Repeated leaks in different rooms over a short period
  • Rust-colored or metallic-tasting water when a tap first runs
  • Pressure that falls at several fixtures
  • Green or white crusting on exposed pipes and joints

None of these proves the pipe has failed, and plenty of old plumbing works for years. A plumber should look at the actual material, joints and access before anyone suggests tearing out walls. If the leak is the only visible sign, leak detection can pin down its source first.

Water heater replacement, and the signs it is time

A tank water heater works until it does not, and the end is often a puddle. Tanks corrode from the inside, and a leaking tank cannot be patched. When the shell goes, water heater replacement is the only real fix.

Warnings come earlier for those who look. Rusty hot water, popping noises from sediment, a tripped relief valve and moisture at the base are all clues. If a family is considering a change of type while the old tank is out, tankless water heaters are worth a conversation about space, gas supply and flow.

Before we arrive in Summit

Shut off the main water valve

Turn the main clockwise where the line enters the house, usually in a basement or utility closet. In a condo or apartment near the station, check under sinks and ask the building manager for the unit or riser valve.

Cut power near wet areas

For a leaking electric water heater, turn off its breaker if the panel is dry. For a gas unit, shut the gas valve only if you can do so safely. If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911.

Clear the area and protect valuables

Move boxes and stored items away from the heater and from walls that are dripping. Remove artwork and rugs from rooms below a leak, and keep children and pets out of wet areas.
