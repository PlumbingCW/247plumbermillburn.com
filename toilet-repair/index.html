Toilet overflowing or leaking right now

An overflowing or leaking toilet is a toilet repair emergency in Millburn, NJ, and the 24/7 dispatch line is open at any hour for it. Reach behind the bowl and turn the supply valve handle clockwise to cut the water, then stop flushing. If the bowl is still rising, lift the tank lid and press the flapper down.

Lay towels at the base, roll up rugs, and keep everyone away from outlets, fans and space heaters near standing water. If other drains gurgle or fill too

When a tub or floor drain also fills as you flush, the blockage is probably in the main drain and not in the toilet. Stop using water in the house and say so on the call. That is a job for drain cleaning, and flushing again only sends more water onto the floor.

Leaks at the base and wax ring failures

Water around the base usually comes from the wax ring or gasket between the toilet and the floor flange. A loose supply connection or a sweating tank can look identical, so dry everything, flush once and watch where the first drops appear. A rocking bowl, a stain on the ceiling below or a soft spot in the floor points to a seal that has been leaking for a while.

The repair means shutting off the water, draining the tank, lifting the toilet and replacing the wax ring or gasket. We reset the bowl, level it and snug the bolts in turns, because overtightening cracks the porcelain. If the subfloor is soft, we say so before going any further.

Signs the base seal has failed

  • Water at the base after a flush
  • A toilet that rocks when you sit
  • A sewer smell in the bathroom
  • Stained flooring or a damp ceiling below
  • Caulk around the base turning dark or loose

Supply line and shutoff valve trouble

The braided line from the wall to the tank fails at its coupling nuts, and when it lets go it spills at full house pressure. Close the main water valve if the stop behind the toilet will not turn. The shutoff valve itself is a common culprit too, since old multi-turn stops drip at the stem or seize solid.

A plumber replaces the line, and the valve when needed, which often means closing the main briefly. Quarter-turn stops are the usual replacement because you can see at a glance whether they are open or shut. Never force a stuck valve with a wrench, since a snapped stem turns a small drip into a flood.

Fill valve and flapper problems

The fill valve refills the tank after each flush, and the flapper seals the tank against the bowl. When the flapper leaks, water trickles into the bowl and the fill valve restarts every so often. A failing fill valve hisses, refills slowly or never shuts off and sends water down the overflow tube.

Mineral-heavy water shortens the life of both parts, because scale stiffens flapper rubber and fouls the fill valve seat. NJ American Water's 2025 report for the Short Hills system lists hardness from 52 to 342 mg/L, and it does not say which streets get which value. Your tap can differ from your neighbor's, so test yours with a store kit or ask the utility.

A running toilet and your water bill

A toilet that runs, even as a thin trickle, can waste a lot of water over a month, and you pay for every gallon without ever seeing a puddle. If the water bill climbs and no faucet drips, a silent flapper leak is the first place to look. The fix is usually a small part, which makes it one of the cheapest ways to cut water use.

A cracked or loose flange

The flange is the ring on the floor pipe that the toilet bolts down to. If it cracks, corrodes or sits too low, the bowl rocks and the seal never holds, no matter how many wax rings you stack. Repair options include a metal repair ring or replacing the damaged section, and a plumber checks the subfloor at the same time.

Clogs a plunger will not clear

A flange plunger and a closet auger clear most bowl clogs, but a toilet that clogs again within days has a deeper problem. Wipes labeled flushable, toys and too much paper are the usual causes, and a partial blockage further down the line behaves the same way. Call for drain cleaning when other fixtures drain slowly or gurgle.
