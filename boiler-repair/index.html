Hot-water or steam, which system do you have

A hot-water boiler heats water and a circulator pump sends it through a closed loop of pipe to radiators, baseboards or in-floor tubing. A steam boiler boils water, and the steam rises to the radiators, gives up its heat and returns as water. A visible glass tube on the side of the boiler that shows a water level usually means steam.

The difference matters because the faults differ. A hot-water system cares about pressure, a working circulator and trapped air. A steam system cares about its water level, its vents and its controls. Do not add water to a steam boiler yourself.

What you can check safely

Safe checks before you call

  • Thermostat set to heat, above room temperature, with fresh batteries if it uses them
  • The boiler's power switch and the breaker for the heating circuit
  • Pressure gauge reading compared with the range marked on the gauge or in the manual
  • Visible water on the floor or at the pipes beside the boiler
  • Whether only one zone is cold or the whole house

Write down what you see. The gauge reading, any error light and which rooms are cold help the dispatcher before a plumber arrives.

What you must not touch

Leave these to a licensed plumber

Do not open the burner compartment, adjust the gas valve, cap or plug the relief valve, bypass a safety control or pour water into a hot steam boiler. These parts protect against fire, scalding and explosion.

A carbon monoxide alarm that sounds is a reason to leave and call 911, not to troubleshoot.

Leaks and relief valve discharge

A boiler that is leaking should be switched off at its power switch, and the water supply to it closed if you can reach the valve. A leak at a fitting, a pump flange or a pipe might be small, but a leak that keeps the pressure low will shut the boiler down. If water is coming from the system, leak detection can trace pipes inside walls or floors.

The relief valve opens to protect the boiler from too much pressure or temperature. A discharge usually points to a waterlogged expansion tank, an overfeeding fill valve or a control that failed to shut the burner off. Never cap the discharge pipe. A valve that drips after it has opened once may also be worn.

Short cycling and bleeding radiators

Short cycling means the boiler fires, shuts off, and fires again within minutes. Causes include a thermostat in a bad spot, a pressure problem, a dirty burner and a boiler too big for the loads it serves. It wears parts quickly, so it is worth a call.

A hot-water radiator that is warm at the bottom and cold at the top has air in it. With the boiler off and cool, open the bleed valve with a radiator key over a cloth until water spits steadily, then close it and check the gauge. Steam radiators use air vents, not bleed valves, so leave those alone.

Annual service is planned work

Servicing a boiler once a year is planned work, scheduled outside the cold weeks. The plumber checks the burner, controls, safeties, pressure and venting, and flushes or tests the water as needed. It catches small faults before they become a January no-heat call.

If a hot-water zone needs new piping, a new radiator or a bathroom addition, see plumbing installation. If the same visit finds trouble with the tank, water heater repair covers it. Dispatch also reaches Summit and the towns around Millburn.
