What to have ready when you call

Quick checklist

  • What you see, hear or smell, in plain words
  • Whether water is still running or has stopped
  • Where your main water shutoff is, if you know
  • Whether anyone smells gas
  • Your street address and ZIP code
  • Who will be home to let the plumber in

None of this needs to be perfect. If you do not know where the shutoff is, say so, and the dispatcher will help you look.

Describing the problem so the dispatcher can help

Leak or burst pipe

Say where the water is coming from and whether a ceiling or wall is wet. That tells the dispatcher whether to walk you to the main valve first.

Drain or sewage

Name the fixtures that are slow or backing up. One sink and a basement floor drain tell different stories about the main line.

Hot water or heat

Say whether a tank or a boiler is involved and whether it runs on gas or electric. A smell of gas changes the order of the call.

Storm water

Say whether the sump pit is rising and whether the pump is running. Lift stored items off the floor while you talk.

Emergency checklist while you wait

Do these safely

  • Close the main water valve for a burst pipe or a leak
  • Stop using drains and washing machines during a backup
  • Stay away from wet outlets, cords and appliances
  • Move valuables and rugs out of the wet area
  • Open a low faucet to drain pressure after the main is closed

Skip drain chemicals during a backup, since they can splash back and create a hazard for the plumber. If it is safe, take photos of the damage, which helps with an insurance claim later.

For a pipe that has already burst, our page on burst pipe repair explains the next steps. A backup is covered under drain cleaning and sewer line repair.
