Emergency plumber dispatch in Millburn, NJ, day or night

Smell gas? Get out first.

Leave the building immediately, without turning anything on or off. Move at least 350 feet away, then call PSE&G at 1-800-880-PSEG (7734) or 911. Reporting a leak is free, and you can call us once the building has been cleared.

When water is coming through a ceiling or a drain is pushing sewage back up, you need an emergency plumber in Millburn, NJ, and you need to reach a real person. Our 24/7 line is answered around the clock, so a Sunday night is handled the same way as a weekday afternoon. A few quick actions will limit the damage while you wait.

Burst pipe or active leak

Close the main water valve, usually where the line enters the basement or utility room, then open a low faucet to drain the pipes. Keep everyone away from wet outlets, and plan on burst pipe repair once the water is off.

Backed-up drain or sewage

Stop all water use, including the washer and dishwasher, because every gallon has nowhere to go. A backup at the lowest drain often points at the main line, which can mean sewer line repair or drain cleaning.

No hot water

Check the breaker or pilot light, and look under the tank for a puddle. A leaking tank usually means water heater replacement, while one that only fails to heat may be a repair.

Sump pump not keeping up

Confirm it has power and that the float moves freely, then lift stored items off the floor. If water keeps rising in the pit, call right away for sump pump repair or replacement.

Millburn and Short Hills plumbing in plain terms

The Short Hills railroad station dates to 1879, built at his own expense by Stewart Hartshorn as he developed the community. Much of Millburn Township has been built up for a long time, so do not assume your pipes are modern. Ask the plumber to identify what your supply and drain lines are made of, and consider repiping if they are corroded.

The West Branch of the Rahway River runs through downtown Millburn. On the night of September 1, 2021, the remnants of Ida pushed it into the streets, and almost eight inches of rain fell in under 24 hours. In the South Mountain area, basements filled with water and sewage.

For a homeowner, that history settles one decision. A sump pump is not an afterthought here, since Millburn also flooded in Hurricane Floyd in 1999 and Hurricane Irene in 2011. Test the pump before storm season, ask about a battery backup, and remember that a pump moves groundwater, not sewage from a backed-up line.

Water quality varies too. NJ American Water's 2025 report for the Short Hills System lists total hardness from 52 to 342 mg/L, above the 250 mg/L secondary limit at the top of that range. The report does not say which streets get which value, so your tap can differ from your neighbor's. Test your own water before you size a softener or blame scale on a failing water heater.
