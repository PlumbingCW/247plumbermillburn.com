Main line blocked and water coming back up

A blocked main is an emergency because every drain in the house empties into it, and our 24/7 line sends a licensed plumber for sewer line cleaning in Millburn, NJ. While you wait, stop using water, keep people away from wet floors and stay clear of standing water near outlets or the panel.

The main sewer line, sometimes called the building drain or lateral, carries all wastewater out of the house. When it clogs, water has nowhere to go. It rises in the lowest drain first and keeps climbing as long as anyone adds more.

Signs the main line is blocked, not one fixture

  • Several fixtures slow or backing up at once
  • A basement floor drain that bubbles or fills
  • A toilet that gurgles when the washer drains
  • Water in the tub when a toilet flushes
  • A sewer smell in the lowest part of the house

When the main line backs up into the house

Sewage in the house is a health issue as well as a plumbing one, and night calls are common because laundry, showers and dishwashers run in the evening. The plumber's job is to open the line first and ask questions second. Same-day visits are available when the schedule allows.

Tell the dispatcher which fixtures are involved and where the water is showing. If you know where the cleanout is, say so. That lets the plumber bring the right cable or jetting head.

A backup is not always your own line. If neighbors report the same problem, or the water shows up only during heavy rain, the public sewer or floodwater may be involved. Say so on the call, because a cleaning at your cleanout may not change the result in that case.

Finding the cleanout

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to the main line. Common places are the basement near where the main leaves the house, a spot along the foundation outside, or a cap in the floor. Look for a round or square plug, often three or four inches wide.

Do not open a cleanout when the line is full and backed up. The water behind the cap can come out with force. Let the plumber open it, and tell them where you think it is.

If you cannot find one, say so when you call. Sometimes the plumber has to pull a toilet or use another opening to get the cable into the line.

What not to do

Mistakes that make a backup worse

  • Do not run water to see if it has cleared, since every gallon adds to the backup
  • Do not pour chemical openers into a full line, because they sit in the pipe and splash back when the line is opened
  • Do not plunge a toilet repeatedly when the main is the problem
  • Do not open a cleanout under pressure
  • Do not wade into sewage near the panel or outlets

A plunger works on a toilet clog but pushes sewage around when the blockage is the main. Chemical openers can also damage old pipe and leave a hazard for the next person who works on the line.

Cable machines cover most first-time clogs. Hydro jetting fits when the pipe wall is coated and the clog keeps returning, and a camera look settles whether the pipe is damaged. When the camera finds a break, the answer is sewer line repair, not another cleaning.

Main sewer and sewer drain cleaning

Main sewer cleaning clears the line from the house to the public connection, and sewer drain cleaning covers the branches that feed it. Both begin with finding the right access point. A main that was cleaned and then drained fine usually needs nothing more, while a main that clogs again in weeks or months points to a defect that drain cleaning of a single fixture will not fix.

A routine cable job does not tell you the condition of the pipe. A camera look afterward turns a cleaning into a diagnosis.
