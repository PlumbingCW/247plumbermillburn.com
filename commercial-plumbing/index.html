When a commercial plumbing problem cannot wait until morning

A burst line behind a restaurant kitchen at 11 p.m. or sewage backing up in a ground-floor office is a commercial plumbing emergency in Millburn, NJ, and the 24/7 dispatch line is open for it. While you wait, close the nearest fixture stop or the building's main water shutoff, keep staff and customers off wet floors, and stop using the affected drains.

Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, which suits problems that are urgent but stable, such as a slow floor sink or a running urinal that can be isolated. If you cannot tell which kind of problem you have, call and describe it. A few questions from the dispatcher usually sort it out.

Water near electrical equipment

Keep everyone out of standing water near panels, outlets, refrigeration compressors and kitchen equipment until the power to that area is off. Shut the water off first if you can reach the valve from dry ground. If you smell gas, leave the building, move at least 350 feet away, then call PSE&G at 1-800-880-PSEG (7734) or 911.

After-hours calls for restaurants, retail and offices

Restaurants generate most of the late calls, because grease, hot water and constant dishwashing punish drains and supply lines all day. A kitchen line that backs up at the end of service is better handled that night than at the next morning's prep. Retail and office buildings call about overflowing restrooms and failed flush valves.

Downtown Millburn has all of those building types close to the river. When the remnants of Ida pushed the West Branch of the Rahway River into the streets in September 2021, mud and sludge covered Millburn Avenue and Main Street, and businesses flooded. A ground-floor business near the water should have floor drains, lift pump and backflow protection checked before storm season.

Big buildings raise the same problems at a larger scale. The Mall at Short Hills covers about 1.4 million square feet and the Paper Mill Playhouse seats 1,200, and we mention them only as examples of building types, not as clients. Anything that size has many restroom groups, long drain runs and zoned shutoffs, so the first useful step is learning which valve isolates which part of the building.

What a commercial leak detection call involves

A commercial leak call starts with questions, not tools. We ask how old the building and its piping are, where the water shows up, whether the meter keeps moving with everything off, and whether the suspect is a supply line, a drain or a roof drain. Those answers decide the first move. Our leak detection work follows the same logic.

Ask about access and shutoffs before the visit. A plumber has to reach the riser room, the mechanical room, the space above restrooms and the cleanouts, and in a leased space the tenant may not hold the keys. Know who can open those doors and who can approve a shutdown, because cutting water to a restaurant or a medical suite affects the people inside.

Large buildings need a look before anyone names a price. A wet ceiling in a three-story office could be a failed fitting, a pinhole in a riser or a stuck fill valve on a rooftop unit. Pressure testing, acoustic listening and camera inspection of drains narrow it down. No method finds everything every time, so we tell you what we found and what we ruled out.

Property managers and landlords

Managers often get the first call from a tenant at 2 a.m. with little information, such as water through the ceiling or a toilet that will not stop running. Give the plumber the tenant's contact, the unit number, where that unit's shutoff is, and who may approve work.

Keep a simple sheet listing every shutoff, the main and each cleanout. A shared drain stack that serves several units is a common reason one tenant's backup floods another's space, and drain cleaning on the stack, not a single fixture, is the fix.

Backflow testing is planned work

A backflow preventer keeps dirty water from being drawn back into the drinking supply, and many commercial properties need theirs tested on a set schedule. This is planned work, not an emergency. Book it for a quiet window, since the water goes off briefly during the test.

A failed test usually points to worn rubber parts inside the assembly. Rules vary by device and use, so ask the Millburn building department or your water utility what your property needs.

Grease traps and storm drains can be scheduled

A grease trap catches fats, oils and grease before they reach the sewer line, and it only works while it has room. Cleaning is routine and can be set on an interval that matches your kitchen's volume. Skip it and the backup arrives at the busiest hour.

Storm drains and catch basins around parking lots and loading areas clog with leaves, silt and trash. Cleaning them is planned work, best done before fall leaf drop and ahead of a forecast storm. Ida dropped almost eight inches of rain on Millburn in under 24 hours, enough to flood a lot with a clogged drain.

If the trap is clean and the building still backs up, the trouble may be further down the line. That is a job for sewer line cleaning.
